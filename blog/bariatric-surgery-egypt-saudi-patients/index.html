Bariatric Surgery in Egypt for Saudi Patients: Eligibility, Real Risks, Cost and Recovery

Weight loss surgery is a serious operation, not a cosmetic shortcut — and the most useful thing anyone can tell you before you travel is what the surgery cannot do, alongside what it can. This guide covers how eligibility is actually assessed, the complications that genuinely occur, what published costs look like in Egypt, and how long you need to stay. If you are researching gastric sleeve or bypass abroad, read the risks section before the price section.

Eligibility is a clinical decision, not a booking decision

Bariatric surgery is assessed against your BMI, associated conditions such as type 2 diabetes or hypertension, your history of previous weight loss attempts, and your general fitness for anaesthesia.

Not everyone who wants surgery is a candidate. The choice between a sleeve and a bypass is made by the surgeon on clinical grounds — never on price, and never because one is more convenient to schedule.

Hayakkom does not assess eligibility. We coordinate access to a licensed bariatric surgeon who does. If the surgeon declines to operate, or recommends a different procedure from the one you had in mind, that is the system working correctly.

The risks, stated plainly

Published complications of sleeve gastrectomy and bypass include bleeding, staple-line leak, infection, long-term nutritional deficiencies requiring lifelong supplementation, and reflux.

Weight regain is common: roughly 10–20% of patients experience significant regain over time.

Excess or loose skin after major weight loss is very common and often needs separate body-contouring procedures later, at additional cost.

Long-term success depends heavily on sustained dietary and behavioural change. The surgery reduces stomach capacity; it does not remove the need to change how you eat. Multidisciplinary programmes that combine surgery with dietetic support and behavioural follow-up produce better outcomes than surgery alone.

Anyone promising you a specific number of kilograms lost is not being straight with you.

What published costs in Egypt look like

Verified aggregator data puts gastric sleeve in Egypt at roughly $1,500–$3,500, with an average around $2,333 based on real invoices across a large sample of clinics. Individual surgeons vary considerably within and beyond that band — some all-inclusive packages are advertised around $2,700, while highly experienced professors quote $5,500–$6,300. One Egyptian surgeon publishes a starting figure from about 38,000 EGP.

These are third-party published figures for context, not a Hayakkom quote. Your actual cost depends on the procedure chosen, the hospital, the length of stay, pre-operative investigations and any complications.

What matters more than the headline number is what the package includes: surgeon fees, anaesthesia, hospital nights, pre-operative tests, and — critically — follow-up. Ask for that itemised in writing before you travel.

How long you need in Egypt

Plan 7–10 days. A realistic sequence is: pre-operative investigations and anaesthetic clearance on arrival, surgery, 1–2 nights in hospital, then monitored recovery before you are cleared to fly.

You will be on a staged diet — liquids, then puréed, then soft food over several weeks. This continues long after you get home.

Do not build a demanding travel itinerary around this procedure. Patients who plan sightseeing in the days after bariatric surgery generally cannot do it, and should not try.

What to prepare before you travel

Recent blood work and any imaging you already have. A list of current medications and doses. Your full weight history and previous attempts. Documentation of associated conditions — diabetes, sleep apnoea, hypertension, joint problems. Details of any previous abdominal surgery. Your height and weight so BMI can be calculated.

Sending these ahead lets the surgeon review your case before you commit to flights, which is the single best way to avoid travelling for a procedure you turn out not to be a candidate for.
