Alexandria: Egypt's Mediterranean city and the gateway to the North Coast

Alexandria offers sea air, history and seafood about 220 km from Cairo — and it is the natural stop on the way to the North Coast resorts in summer.

In short: Alexandria is Egypt's main Mediterranean city, about 220 km north-west of Cairo — roughly 2.5 hours by fast train or by road. Families come for the Corniche, the Bibliotheca Alexandrina, the Qaitbay Citadel, Montaza's gardens and fresh seafood, and many combine two or three nights here with a stay on the North Coast (Sahel). Hayakkom coordinates hotels, a driver and guides with licensed Egyptian companies, in Arabic and English.

Why Gulf families like Alexandria

Alexandria feels different from Cairo: a long seafront, a cooler breeze and a slower pace. In summer, the average high in August, the hottest month, is about 30°C, and the hot-weather season runs roughly from June to mid-October (WeatherSpark). That makes it a comfortable summer base compared with inland Egypt, and a pleasant city break in spring and autumn.

The Corniche

The Corniche curves along the Eastern Harbour and beyond — the best way to feel the city. Families walk it at sunset, stop for ice cream and fresh juice, and watch the fishing boats. With a driver you can follow it from the city centre east towards Montaza, stopping at the viewpoints along the way. Choose a sea-facing hotel room if you can; the view is a large part of the stay.

Bibliotheca Alexandrina

The modern library, inaugurated on 16 October 2002 near the site of the ancient Library of Alexandria, is more than a reading room (Bibliotheca Alexandrina). Inside are museums including an Antiquities Museum and a Manuscript Museum, a planetarium, art galleries and permanent exhibitions. It is a good half-day with older children; check opening days and any visitor rules before you go.

Qaitbay Citadel

Sultan Qaitbay built this fortress between 1477 and 1479 on the site of the famous Lighthouse of Alexandria, at the entrance to the Eastern Harbour (Citadel of Qaitbay). Children enjoy the walls, towers and sea views; adults enjoy the story. Late afternoon light is lovely here, and the seafood restaurants of the Anfushi area are close by for dinner.

Montaza gardens

East of the centre, the former royal estate of Montaza sits above a Mediterranean beach. Its palaces date from 1892 and 1932, and the large gardens are now a public park (Montaza Palace). It is a green, shaded place for a family walk, a picnic or simply a quieter afternoon by the sea.

Seafood and Alexandrian food

Alexandria is known for fish chosen fresh and grilled or fried to order, with rice, salads and tahini. Many restaurants let you pick your fish by weight — ask the price per kilo before ordering. We can suggest family-friendly places. Do also try the city's sweets and its famous liver sandwiches if the children are adventurous.

Two days in Alexandria: a simple plan

  1. Day 1: the Bibliotheca Alexandrina in the morning, a light lunch, Qaitbay Citadel in the late afternoon, then seafood in Anfushi and a walk on the Corniche.
  2. Day 2: the Catacombs of Kom el-Shoqafa for history lovers, Montaza gardens in the afternoon, then on to the North Coast or back to Cairo.

With small children, keep midday for rest at the hotel.

Gateway to the North Coast

The North Coast (Sahel) resorts stretch west of Alexandria along the Mediterranean, and in summer they are where many Egyptian and Gulf families spend their holidays. A common plan is two nights in Alexandria for the city, then five nights in a resort or a private chalet on the coast. Read our North Coast guide and compare the North Coast summer package below.

Getting there and getting around

From Cairo, the faster trains take about 2.5 hours, and the drive is roughly the same, depending on traffic (PlanetWare). For families with luggage, a private car with a driver is usually easier, and it stays with you for the Corniche and Montaza. We arrange it with a licensed Egyptian transport company, which operates and invoices the service: a sedan with driver 375–560 SAR per day or a family van 790–1,125 SAR per day. Questions people ask

How far is Alexandria from Cairo?

About 220 km. The faster trains take around 2.5 hours, and a private car takes roughly the same depending on traffic. A driver is usually easiest for families with luggage.

When is the best time to visit Alexandria?

For the sea and beaches, June to mid-October, with August averaging about 30°C. Spring and autumn are pleasant for sightseeing. Winter is cooler and can be rainy and windy.

Should we stay in Alexandria or on the North Coast?

Many families do both: two nights in Alexandria for the Corniche, the library, Qaitbay and seafood, then a resort or chalet on the North Coast for a calm beach week.
